# Cross-case and mixed-method synthesis

Read for multiple cases, mixed methods, evolving coding or corrections. Use the parts material to the requested decision. The purpose is explainable synthesis, not a mandatory research database or a fixed number of themes.

## Preserve the unit and the episode

Identify the analytic unit before comparing: an organization, person, task episode, interaction, transaction or cohort may answer different questions. Retain nesting and source lineage. A call transcript, its summary and a strategy deck citing it belong to the same source family. Several people reporting one event can corroborate its details without multiplying its occurrence.

Reconstruct the case before extracting codes: context, attempted progress, choices, constraint, result, source medium and quality limits. Distinguish a behavior occurring from a question eliciting a claim about it. If only a summary is supplied, do not invent the underlying dialogue, exact wording or sequence. Keep source locators fine enough to recover context, including material that contradicts the leading account.

Do not silently merge respondents by similar descriptions or assume different IDs identify different people. Mark ambiguous identity and explain which comparisons remain usable. One case may occupy different roles or job situations; avoid a permanent persona label that erases the contrast.

## Make coding reviseable and inspectable

Use concepts that answer the research question, allowing both question-led and evidence-emergent codes. A small analysis may need annotations rather than a formal codebook. For a larger or iterated corpus, preserve:

- A concept ID/name, definition and the phenomenon it describes.
- Inclusion/exclusion boundaries, examples and counterexamples with locators.
- Code level: directly reported/observed behavior, situational condition, interpreted mechanism or researcher question. Do not mix those levels into one frequency.
- Unit of assignment, versions and the reason for a merge, split or definition change.

When a definition changes, revisit previously coded material affected by the change, or explicitly mark its old coding as not comparable pending recoding. Do not compare frequencies across coding versions as though the category were unchanged. Disagreement between analysts is a prompt to inspect context and assumptions, not a majority-vote truth rule. Preserve a useful rival interpretation even when the leading coding scheme cannot accommodate it.

Track not asked, asked with no answer, unclear/damaged, explicit negative, qualified answer and positive evidence separately where question-level coverage matters. An absent code may mean not observed or not elicited, not that the condition was absent. Multiple mentions by one respondent are not multiple customers. Post-concept interest and spontaneous problem accounts require separate interpretation.

## Compare for mechanisms, not just recurrence

Use a compact case-by-concept matrix when it makes contrasts visible. Include actor/job, relevant conditions, source-linked behavior, result and collection limits. Preserve disconfirming cells and missingness instead of filling gaps with the dominant story.

Useful comparisons include:

- Same apparent behavior, different constraints or intended outcomes.
- Similar conditions, different choices or outcomes.
- Same customer across situations or time.
- Successful workaround versus abandonment, deliberate non-use or no workaround.
- Different actors in the same job, especially across a handoff or payment/approval boundary.

Explain why a comparison is informative and what confounds it. These are qualitative contrasts, not automatically matched causal designs. Recurrence can establish a pattern within inspected material; causal attribution, population reach and severity require additional support. A rare case may expose an important failure mode without showing its frequency.

For a candidate mechanism, articulate the situation → constraint or incentive → choice → consequence. Link the observed parts to sources and mark the inferred links. Generate plausible rivals only when they would change interpretation; do not invent contrarian alternatives to fill a checklist. State the specific evidence that separates them, not merely “do more research.”

## Join qualitative and quantitative evidence without laundering uncertainty

For each consequential mixed-method claim, identify what each method contributes and its limit. Numeric data may establish a scoped distribution or temporal pattern while an episode suggests a mechanism; combining them does not automatically prove the mechanism at population scale.

Before comparing, align:

- Entity/unit and whether identity linkage is actually supported and permitted.
- Event, eligibility and metric definitions; inclusion/exclusion and deduplication.
- Population/frame, recruitment/selection, exposure and observation window.
- Data freshness, missing or truncated outputs, instrumentation changes and known proxy weaknesses.
- Numerator/denominator and the executed calculation or reported source of the number.

If linkage is absent, compare at the supported level rather than attributing an aggregate pattern to specific interviewees. If cohort ages differ, an apparent adoption/retention difference may be observation opportunity. If the sample is selected for unusual behavior, do not use its interview recurrence as prevalence. Numbers derived from the same events are not independent corroboration merely because separate charts display them.

Keep convergence, complementarity and contradiction distinct. Agreement can still share a bad definition or source. Contradiction may reveal a real contextual difference or a measurement problem; preserve both until the evidence resolves it. Temporal sequence alone does not remove confounding. Use causal language only within the limits of an appropriate supplied design and evidence; otherwise report association, candidate explanation and needed discrimination.

When a potential insight has an economic implication, distinguish measured reach/effort from modeled assumptions and avoid extrapolating one anecdote to all customers. Request the missing input and state the decision that depends on it. This skill identifies the implication; it does not approve a price, financial forecast or investment.

## Determine what changed and what follows

Compare the explanation to the supplied baseline rather than to an invented naive belief. Record confirmed, narrowed, contradicted, newly raised or unresolved understanding with its source. Without a baseline, the finding can still be valuable; its novelty is unknown. Do not award novelty merely because the title is surprising.

A latent-need hypothesis should connect observed behavior or an evidenced constraint to intended progress and an unmet consequence, then name a rival explanation and distinguishing evidence. An inconvenience, unrequested feature, missing dashboard event or competitor capability alone is insufficient. Include satisfactory alternatives and rational non-use as live interpretations.

Carry forward implications at the appropriate boundary: customer/job understanding; missing research perspective; baseline service expectation; capability or access gap; incentive mismatch; reliability symptom; value/cost allocation; or a genuinely new capability hypothesis. Do not rank a feature backlog, choose a solution, set dates or infer authorization to investigate/fix a product defect.

## Correction impact

Use a lightweight dependency trail for material findings: source/definition → coded observation → interpretation → current headline → implication → supplied dependent artifact. When a source, denominator or capability assertion changes, revisit the affected links. Record which were reassessed, which remain unresolved and which artifacts were not supplied.

Preserve the original episode and coding history. Replace or qualify the contradicted current conclusion within the returned analysis rather than leaving a correction buried under an unchanged headline. If a previous report needs editing, identify the exact affected claim and proposed revision; do not claim the report is updated without the authorized edit. A corrected technical fact can redirect the explanation from “missing capability” to an unresolved access, awareness or experience question without proving any of those rivals.
