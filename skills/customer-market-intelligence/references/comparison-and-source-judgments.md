# Comparison and source judgments

Use these checks for consequential market comparisons; do not fill missing fields with plausible values.

## Comparable claims

Record a material claim's source ID and exact locator, source type, publication/effective date when available, retrieval date if actually known, geography, segment, edition/plan, configuration and permitted use. Do not infer a retrieval date from a file's modification time. A source can be authoritative about its advertised capability without establishing customer adoption or outcomes.

Keep distinct:

- Vendor documentation or pricing: what the vendor describes for the stated scope and date.
- Observed product use or a verified test: what occurred under the inspected conditions.
- Customer account: what that person reports knowing, choosing or experiencing.
- Analyst reconstruction or search excerpt: a lead with the supplied coverage, not automatically the underlying primary source.

A vendor's own page is not proof that its competitive superlatives or outcome claims are true. Several pages repeating one announcement are not independent corroboration. A negative customer experience need not disprove that a capability exists, but it can challenge its accessibility or fitness in that situation.

## Pricing and total burden

Compare prices only after identifying the charging unit, currency, geography/taxes, billing period, commitment, inclusions, overages, minimums and required add-ons when material. A dated promotion, annualized monthly figure, negotiated quote and current list price are not interchangeable. Keep unknown terms visible; do not use remembered prices to fill them.

Normalize only with supplied or verified conversion inputs and show the arithmetic and date. If compatible inputs are unavailable, present scoped original figures without pretending they are directly comparable. Do not replace a range/quote-only price with a guessed midpoint. Include material switching, setup, labor and risk burdens separately; these are not automatically cash expenses or additive.

Current fact requested but only old source supplied: state what was true according to that source, what cannot be called current, and the exact edition/region/date check needed through authorized acquisition. Absence from a pricing page does not establish free availability, exclusion or contractual entitlement.

## Market structure without a fabricated market size

Choose the unit relevant to the customer job: people, organizations, locations, workflows or transactions. Avoid treating those units as equivalent. A registered account count can include dormant, duplicated, ineligible or subaccount entities. Industry spending may include jobs the proposed approach cannot address.

Separate evidence about:

- Who experiences the job/problem and under which circumstances.
- Who has authority, budget and a reason to change.
- Who can actually be reached and served under current constraints.
- Who might adopt and sustain use; this remains uncertain without suitable evidence.

Do not manufacture market share, growth rates or a bottom-up TAM from an interview count. If a source's population, method or period is unavailable, limit its use accordingly. Market-structure arguments should identify the mechanism—for example distribution dependence or a complementary product's bargaining position—not merely name a framework.

## Interpreting gaps

An unserved space can reflect unmet demand, low frequency, weak economics, a hidden substitute, adoption friction, an unavailable complement, or an unacceptable trade-off. State what observation would separate the relevant explanations. An existing feature may shift the question from invention to discoverability, trust or workflow fit; it does not automatically erase the customer problem.

Table stakes are context-specific expectations with evidence, not a universal list copied from competitors. Distinguish parity needed for consideration from outcomes that influence actual choice and reasons an alternative remains good enough. Neither implies a roadmap priority.

For a consequential correction, show the old premise, corrected scope/source, which comparison changes, which inference survives, and what must be reopened. If only one edition changed, do not generalize that correction across all editions or customer contexts.
