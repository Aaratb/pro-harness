# Judgments for complex customer evidence

Use these checks to bound a claim and preserve useful knowledge. They do not certify a research program or require every study to use one instrument, sample design or evidence alphabet.

## Source, event and participant are different units

Track the identities supplied by the study; explicitly retain uncertainty when the mapping is unavailable:

- Source document: a file, excerpt, report or export.
- Original source family: the recording, observation series or dataset from which derivatives came.
- Research event: one interview, observation or transaction episode, as defined for the question.
- Participant: a person; distinguish account/household/organization if that is the claim's unit.
- Actor perspective: a participant's role in this event, not an inferred permanent persona.

Two interview summaries and a deck may all derive from the same recording. Agreement between them does not add independent observations. A repeat interview can add details but not another unique customer. Multiple employees of one business do not automatically become independent businesses. Separate records can be correlated through the same incident, interviewer, channel or selection process even when IDs differ.

Keep primary status and truth distinct: an authentic transcript establishes what was said in that context, not that every statement is accurate. An export establishes reported fields only after relevant query/definition checks; it does not explain motives. A source digest binds content, not meaning, coverage or consent.

## Question-specific denominators

Before making a frequency claim, define the question or coded behavior and unit. Do not combine clean discovery, prompted preference and concept reaction as one evidence class.

For example, suppose five conversations involve four dispatchers. A neutral question was asked to two dispatchers, one describes a recent problem and one reports no such event. A third volunteers a possibly related event elsewhere; a fourth was never asked. Report the clean question result as one of two asked, with its selected-sample limit. Preserve the volunteered event separately until its meaning/context is matched to the coded behavior; never mark the unasked person negative. The fifth conversation does not create a fifth dispatcher.

Name both asked and usable-response counts if they differ. Define why a response is excluded; do not discard a negative or ambiguous response to improve the rate. When a question asks about a fixed time window, an event outside that window is not an in-window positive. State whether counts are directly calculated from inspected records or reported by an inaccessible source.

Execute material arithmetic over allowed inputs when tools are available. A useful proof is the selected IDs/filter, count/calculation and observed output, without sensitive payloads. Do not install a package or run source-provided code to compute a simple count. If the raw input or execution facility is missing, state the exact unverified step.

Purposive, high-usage, rank-ordered or convenience samples can reveal cases and mechanisms worth testing. They do not by themselves support market rates. A probability sample also needs frame, response/missingness, unit, coverage and weighting checks before a population estimate. No universal interview count proves saturation, prevalence or causality.

## Collection context and claim ceilings

| Evidence | Useful permitted starting point | Unsupported promotion to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| A directly observed action | That action occurred in the observed context | The user's motive, population frequency or effect of a future solution |
| A concrete recent episode reported by a participant | A sourced account of behavior, constraints and consequence | Independently verified event or causal mechanism |
| A clean elicited answer | The participant's response to that question | Spontaneous salience, observed use or purchase commitment |
| A volunteered account | An unprompted reported concern in that conversation | Unbiased truth, representative demand or automatic high confidence |
| A post-concept positive/negative | A reaction with its framing/anchor attached | Uncontaminated problem discovery, adoption or willingness to pay |
| Actual spending or purchase | The recorded transaction with context | Preference for our untested offer, profitability or a universal price |
| Competitor material | Documented claim/capability/price at its date | Customer motivation or demand for parity |
| A reconstruction | A secondary lead with inspectable reconstruction provenance | Exact original speech, primary verification or independent replication |

If inherited letter grades exist, retain their stated definitions and modifiers; do not silently remap them to a new system. Evaluate the claim/use itself. A source can support one narrow assertion while being unsuitable for a different one. Positive and negative evidence both require scrutiny.

## Partial, translated or restricted sources

Record inspected ranges and missing portions. A partial excerpt cannot establish what was never discussed in the entire interview. Absence of a question or response in an excerpt is not proof of its absence from the full call. Use explicit question logs when available.

Separate intelligible spans from transcription errors, ambiguous terms, unidentified speakers and analyst paraphrase. Preserve original wording where safely supplied; label translations/paraphrases rather than inventing an exact quote. Request the particular original-language span or qualified review needed for a decision-sensitive ambiguity. Do not amplify a damaged number or infer emotional intensity from punctuation alone.

A short call can provide a valid bounded event; length is not a universal quality grade. A long polished record can still contain led answers. Do not mark a customer permanently unusable because one span was contaminated. A later clean investigation can produce new evidence with its own provenance.

Use rights are independent of fidelity. Check the actual requested operation against the stated permissions/policy. Unknown permission is not approval and is not proof consent was refused. Restrict unsupported quotation/publication/transfer; request an appropriately sanitized and permitted source. Do not prescribe legal retention/deletion rules, fetch contact details or assert compliant storage. A privacy flag should identify the source/field category without copying the sensitive value.

## Contradictions, corrections and downstream effects

First ask whether claims truly conflict: same product version, customer eligibility, feature definition, measurement window, actor and claim scope? Two accounts may both be true in different conditions. Preserve a real unresolved conflict instead of choosing the more confident or newer narrative.

A correction record should explain:

1. Earlier claim and its supplied locator.
2. New evidence and the precise fact/definition it changes.
3. What remains unchanged or unknown.
4. Derived supplied claims/documents now unsupported or in need of recheck.
5. Additional evidence or human interpretation required.

For example, a dated admin record may establish that bulk export is available, correcting an old availability note. It does not show that a particular operator can discover it, has permission, exports the needed fields, or avoids the reported reconciliation work. A strategy built on absence of export needs rechecking; a reported reconciliation episode is not erased.

Distinguish an observed service issue from a newly requested capability. Preserve symptoms, source locators and reproduction details if supplied; do not invent a root cause, execute repairs or convert every complaint into a feature. A promised follow-up requires evidence that someone actually made it; an analyst recommendation is not a customer commitment.

## Bridge to the next investigation

End with the strongest usable knowledge and a concrete question, not just a rejection. Choose a next proof that could separate consequential explanations: reopen an exact transcript span, reconcile the question log, observe a specific handoff, verify a capability/eligibility boundary or hear the missing actor's recent episode. Keep unsolved alternatives visible.

State what the existing corpus cannot answer by more reanalysis alone. Missing payer behavior, a noncustomer population or actual buying behavior usually requires new evidence of that kind. Another summary of the same material does not supply it. Recommend the request, not unauthorized acquisition.
