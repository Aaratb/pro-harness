# Customer fit and alignment

Make the customer focus and the conditions under which the promise holds inspectable. Do not infer alignment merely because documents repeat the same target label. Do not force a final ICP or a polished positioning statement when the evidence leaves rival theses open.

## Name the unit and the proposed focus

Distinguish the customer segment (a useful grouping), situational persona (a person's role, behavior and constraints), ICP (a revisable best-fit thesis) and research cohort (whom it is informative and feasible to study). They can inform one another but are not interchangeable. In B2B, specify the account/use context separately from operators, beneficiaries, champions, buyers, payers and approvers. In consumer work, do not invent organization fields; one person can occupy several situations.

Use observable fit conditions, why they matter and the supplied basis. Distinguish known mismatch, unresolved fit and absence from the research frame. A promising segment can have an unsolved delivery problem; that does not make its need invalid. Conversely, an enthusiastic customer can be costly to serve, unable to buy or atypical. High revenue, a demographic resemblance or a persuasive interview is not a complete fit argument. Keep research contrasts outside the proposed ICP when they could overturn it.

## Trace the alignment chain

Use a compact matrix or connected prose, proportional to the decision. For each consequential proposition make these links explicit:

| Link | Question and evidence |
|---|---|
| Situation and progress | Which actor in which episode needs to accomplish what, why does it matter, and how is that known? |
| Proposed ICP | Which observable conditions make this a plausible fit; who is outside it or unknown; current-fit or future-fit? |
| Role and buying path | Who uses, benefits, decides, pays or blocks adoption? Is the message addressing the person whose job it describes? |
| Promise and position | What outcome and comparison are communicated, in what category/context? What might the customer reasonably infer? |
| Delivery mechanism | Which version and capability can actually deliver the promise, with what assistance, delay, exceptions and prerequisites? What is only proposed? |
| Alternative and difference | What do customers do instead, including manual work or no change? Is the difference useful to this job or merely a parity claim? |
| Adoption and economics | What must change, who bears switching/service burden, how is value realized/captured, and which costs or incentives remain unknown? |
| Evidence and falsifier | Which source/locator supports or conflicts with each link, what inference is permitted, and what observation could reverse the thesis? |

Missing data is not a contradiction. A direct contradiction is not resolved by adding a vague confidence caveat. Mark each important link supported at a stated scope, conflicting or unknown in ordinary language; no aggregate alignment score, invented cutoff or mandatory traffic-light gate. Describe coherence and evidence sufficiency separately. A perfectly coherent untested hypothesis is still untested.

Treat aggregate and interview evidence at their actual units, selection/exposure and time windows. Several champions in one account do not establish several independent customers. A selected success cohort cannot prove market prevalence or an absence of poor-fit customers. A cost scenario is not a measured margin, and time saved is not automatically willingness to pay.

## Resolve tensions without choosing delivery

When the chain fails, identify which premise needs revision rather than automatically moving the customer to fit the product:

- A real need plus absent capability can remain a **future-fit opportunity hypothesis**. Do not market that capability as available or select it for building.
- A supported current capability may warrant a narrower **current-fit proposition**, with service requirements and limits visible. It may still lack proof of demand or profitable delivery.
- The customer may be right but the message may imply the wrong role, outcome, speed or degree of automation. Propose a clearer hypothesis and the evidence needed to test it; analyst editing is not a comprehension result.
- A distinct capability may be irrelevant to the job or already acceptably served by an alternative. Parity, service/process/awareness changes, partnership or no new product remain legitimate conclusions.
- A promise can require more assurance or assistance than its low-touch economics allow. Expose the conflict and the missing value/cost evidence; do not silently invent a price, staffing plan or margin.

Explain genuine sacrifices and competing theses with conditions that would favor each. Selecting the next question to learn from is not selecting the roadmap, revenue target, price, channel, staffing, architecture or launch.

## Correction and handoff

A capability correction, changed ICP membership rule, revised buyer role, message version or economic assumption can invalidate multiple links. Preserve the original and correction provenance, identify which current headlines/claims must be retracted, narrowed or left unresolved, and retain authentic historical episodes. Newer records require an appropriate basis; recency alone is not authority.

List affected supplied ICP, positioning, strategy, message stimulus, study interpretation and downstream handoff artifacts. Identify unavailable artifacts as uninspected. Do not claim any were edited, approved or deployed. A changed message cannot retroactively change what participants understood from the earlier one.

Return an evidence-qualified strategic choice for human consideration, its remaining disagreements, exact next proof and non-decisions. Customer Backward Pro defines and tests the early fit thesis; GTM can later operationalize it and feed back evidence. Neither document alignment nor clear language certifies product-market fit.
