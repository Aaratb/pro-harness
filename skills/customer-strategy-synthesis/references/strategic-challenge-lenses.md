# Strategic challenge lenses

Use only the lenses that interrogate a load-bearing part of the thesis. Do not manufacture missing competitive facts or demand.

## From assets to defensible customer value

For a claimed advantage, trace the causal argument:

1. What asset/access/capability is actually evidenced, and for which scope?
2. What does it allow the business to do differently for this customer job?
3. Why would that difference change a real choice or sustained use?
4. What would an alternative need to replicate, substitute or neutralize it?
5. Under what conditions could the benefit or capture persist?

Label unsupported links as hypotheses. Existing distribution may lower contact effort without creating trust or conversion. Proprietary data may lack relevance, quality, permission or a feedback mechanism. Switching friction may retain reluctant customers without improving value. A brand advantage in one context may not transfer to a different buyer or job.

Do not claim network effects from user growth alone: explain who gains from whose participation and what evidence supports that mechanism. Do not claim a learning loop from data accumulation alone: name the permitted feedback, improvement and customer benefit. These are candidate mechanisms, not required strategies.

## Coherence and sacrifice

Check whether the value promise, customer circumstances, adoption path, required activities and economics can hold together. Examples of tensions worth examining when supported include an assurance-heavy promise with a low-touch service assumption, a frictionless claim that requires organizational migration, or a premium claim where the beneficiary cannot authorize payment.

A sacrifice must follow from the thesis: excluded use contexts, outcomes not optimized, trade-offs accepted or approaches not pursued. Do not invent exclusions just to sound focused or confuse a strategic exclusion with an approved roadmap cancellation.

Keep distinct the strategic question (where/how value could be created and sustained), research question (what would verify that thesis), portfolio decision (which opportunities receive investment and when) and architecture decision (how a chosen capability is built). This skill owns the first two as proposals only.

## Competitor and ecosystem response

Use available evidence to consider plausible responses: matching a feature, bundling, changing price, controlling distribution, partnering or targeting a different segment. Identify which response would matter and why; do not predict an incumbent's intent as fact. Test whether the thesis survives a reasonable substitute, not only the weakest known competitor.

Ask who else must change behavior, who bears new cost or loses value, and whether incentives align. Existing suppliers, channels, approvers and frontline operators may affect adoption more than a feature comparison. A strategy dependent on an unverified partner permission remains conditional.

## Nonlinear scale and timing

Define what changes 10x/100x relative to a named baseline: customer count, diversity, transaction intensity, service load, geographic reach or desired customer improvement. Avoid conflating a tenfold market with a tenfold customer benefit.

Check whether the customer mix, problem mechanism, trust requirements, cost structure, distribution access or partner constraints change qualitatively. Consider whether a manual/service approach remains viable and whether a software approach creates a new burden, without selecting an architecture or staffing plan. Scaling constraints can narrow the proposed arena rather than justify premature infrastructure.

A why-now claim needs a mechanism and a dated source: what changed, which customer/actor it affects, and why it alters adoption or economics. If those are missing, retain a timing hypothesis instead of inventing a market inflection.

## Falsifiers that matter

A useful falsifier contradicts a load-bearing implication, not merely a cosmetic metric. It could show that the alleged gap is already acceptably served, that the beneficiary cannot influence purchase, that expected value is not realized after switching effort, or that the claimed advantage does not change choices. Name the observable episode/measure and the scope where it applies.

Do not set arbitrary pass percentages. Use supplied business constraints, relevant baseline evidence or a justified study design. If calibration is missing, specify how to establish it and keep the strategic conclusion provisional. A skeptical challenge does not create new customer evidence; retain source lineage when incorporating it.
