# Candidate relationships and scope

Read for substantive expansion, overlapping ideas, multiple jobs, umbrella initiatives or corrected evidence. These lenses guide judgment; they are not a required checklist or output template.

## 1. Opportunity is not the requested solution

Use a source-linked chain: business intent → actor/job/situation → obstacle or unmet progress → plausible explanation → intervention mechanism → expected change. Label inferred links. A shorter chain is valid when evidence is thin; do not fabricate the middle to connect a favored feature to revenue.

The buyer's gain may create work for the operator or disadvantage another customer. State that tension. Similar complaints can come from different mechanisms, while different symptoms can share a bottleneck. Keep these distinctions available to prioritization without choosing the strategic winner.

A useful rival explanation predicts a different response to intervention. If people cannot find a capability, adding the same capability again differs from making it visible. If they are not eligible for it, visibility alone may not help. If the workaround succeeds at low cost, a new feature may not address a material gap.

When no business objective was supplied, explain customer progress and ask for strategic context only if needed for the requested comparison. Do not invent a north-star metric or ICP to fill cells.

## 2. Mechanism diversity, not naming diversity

Compare the actor/action, decision or handoff being changed, information made available, constraint relaxed and expected consequence. A channel/timing/visual variant may be meaningfully different under some conditions; state the condition rather than treating every variant as either unique or redundant.

For a missed-appointment problem, another reminder, a customer change request, a staffed reply process, clearer existing change instructions and standby-slot use may intervene at different points. They are candidate mechanisms, not a universally recommended five or a claim that all are needed. Traffic, travel uncertainty or repair constraints could make some ineffective.

For each consequential new idea, give the causal hypothesis in ordinary language and the strongest relevant limit. An AI label or sophisticated implementation is not an additional mechanism unless its changed behavior is explained. Do not claim a generated idea has customer backing merely because it shares a JTBD label.

Generate broadly when requested, but stop adding near-duplicates once they no longer expand the choice space. Record remaining search gaps instead of padding. Do not silently narrow to the most attractive few; retain breadth for human consideration.

## 3. Preserve identity while making relationships legible

- **Duplicate mechanism:** same intended action/outcome under the same conditions. Keep original IDs; propose a grouping or merge for human review.
- **Variant:** a meaningful difference in actor, condition, surface or degree; explain when it matters.
- **Substitute:** alternative ways to achieve the same relevant progress; choosing both may or may not add value.
- **Complement:** capabilities that increase one another's usefulness; not necessarily mandatory.
- **Enabler:** a capability/condition required for a stated option to deliver its minimum intended outcome; cite why. Separate existing enablers from new work and unverified assumptions.
- **Shared benefit:** several candidates claim the same affected population or outcome. Flag possible double counting without inventing an aggregate impact.
- **Dependency or incompatibility:** identify its direction, scope and evidence. A user-journey prerequisite is not yet an engineering implementation sequence.

One candidate can relate to several problems and one problem to several candidates. Preserve those mappings; do not force a one-problem/one-feature tree. Labels are not evidence or selection.

## 4. Comparable feature boundaries

A feature should convey a recognizable capability or improvement in a customer's situation. A title such as “operations platform” may conceal independent workflows, adoption prerequisites and reporting. Flag that mismatch before comparing it with a one-surface improvement.

Offer alternative boundaries: minimal coherent customer progress; an extended experience; or an umbrella with proposed child capabilities. Show what each excludes and whether the smaller option still works for the stated audience. Do not equate minimal with thin to the point of uselessness.

Keep proposed children linked to the source parent. Do not count both parent and children as independently delivered value, quietly turn five umbrella labels into dozens of selected commitments, or convert a tentative decomposition into approved scope. Detailed flows, acceptance cases, technical architecture and engineering sizing belong later.

A known prerequisite may make an option incomplete without another item, but that is a question for later human portfolio comparison—not permission to add the prerequisite to the selected set.

## 5. Coverage, table stakes and strategic fit

Check which supplied jobs, segments, lifecycle moments and legitimate exceptions have no candidate or only one favored mechanism. Include buyer/operator distinctions where they affect adoption. Do not require research across every conceivable segment.

Distinguish baseline expectations, reliability/service obligations, adoption/awareness gaps and potential differentiation. These labels need context; competitor presence or a loud customer request alone does not establish a universal table stake. Neither category predetermines priority.

When a candidate conflicts with the supplied ICP/value promise, explain the exact conflict and who might still benefit. Do not quietly redraw the ICP or exclude contrary evidence to make the backlog coherent. Human strategy can be challenged with evidence, not rewritten by a normalization pass.

## 6. Correction and reconsideration

Record the old premise, new source, what the correction actually covers and remaining uncertainty. Distinguish “the product has this capability” from “this customer had access, knew about it and successfully used it.”

Reopen affected problem diagnoses and relationships. A broad missing-feature hypothesis may become a narrower eligibility gap, a messaging option or an unresolved service issue. Keep the historical experience and unchanged candidates intact.

Retain human-selected/deferred labels with an explicit reconsideration flag where needed. A good proposed alternative does not become selected. Do not claim to edit unavailable Product Notes, PRDs or decisions; identify them for their owners.

## 7. What weak output looks like

- Ten polished titles with one underlying intervention.
- Every original idea “validated” by a copied summary of the same interviews.
- A blank priority filled because the agent found the idea attractive.
- An initiative compared with a small feature without exposing scope asymmetry.
- New software inferred where existing capability plus service may suffice.
- A corrected capability dismissed as proof that the historical customer problem never existed.
- A clean table that discards unnamed-cell content, source rows or inconvenient exceptions.

The repair is more faithful reasoning, not automatically more rows, longer text or additional mandatory skills.
