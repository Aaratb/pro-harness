# Decision Episodes and Conditional Cases

Read the sections relevant to the model being explained. These patterns help distinguish decision mechanisms; they are not a checklist every customer must satisfy.

## Find the event worth reconstructing

A routine product-use trigger is not necessarily a buying trigger. A dispatcher prepares a schedule each morning; reconsidering the scheduling arrangement may happen after a missed visit, an expansion, a contract change, a new constraint, or no observable trigger at all. Do not presume a crisis is required. Trace what made the alternative worth considering in the supplied case.

Useful episode anchors include the last paid choice, a comparison that ended in no change, a trial that never reached use, a migration that stalled after approval, or a renewal completed without enthusiastic use. Establish the relevant before/after state and who witnessed which parts. An episode reconstructed from notes remains a reported reconstruction.

The explanatory chain is:

`changed situation → desired progress → credible alternatives → commitment conditions → implementation → actual progress or aftermath`

When a link is unknown, explain its importance and the discriminating observation. Do not infer a completed chain from the presence of its endpoints. A invoice plus usage log does not reveal who approved the purchase or why.

## Match roles to the actual buying model

### Individual and household decisions

Begin with the person choosing and using the offer. Split roles only when the source makes that split consequential: someone else pays, a caregiver helps implement, a shared device affects access, or another household member bears the ongoing work. Do not insert a champion, security reviewer or procurement owner into a direct individual purchase.

Price sensitivity is not a personality trait. A person may afford an offer yet prefer a free alternative for an infrequent job; another may pay despite modest means because that specific outcome matters. An abandoned checkout might reflect payment failure, interruption, unclear value, a deliberate decision or another cause. Use the supplied context to identify plausible explanations, not an invented internal monologue.

The next proof might be a recent choice between this offer and the actual workaround, followed through use. A favorable concept reaction establishes neither payment nor benefit. If the offer includes another person's information or cooperation, identify that dependency without assuming permission.

### Organizational decisions

Distinguish the person who can authorize spend from people whose cooperation is necessary for implementation. A gatekeeper may evaluate a real condition without deciding commercial value. A senior title does not establish final authority; a person called a champion may have no ability to fund or implement the change.

Look for local versus system-wide outcomes: the operator saves time but finance acquires reconciliation work, management receives visibility but partners bear data-entry costs, or the buyer benefits while users cannot access the necessary workflow. Explain how that tradeoff could produce delay or refusal. Do not presume that more executive persuasion is the remedy.

Separate recorded requirements from unknowns: an actual renewal date differs from an assumed annual buying cycle; a quoted assurance request differs from an invented compliance blocker. If approval is complete but adoption stalls, investigate implementation or incentive dependencies before declaring a buying failure.

### Marketplaces, partners and multi-party offers

Analyze each relevant side's decision and the dependency between them. Demand interest does not establish supply availability; a signed partner agreement does not establish the operational coverage needed for a specific customer promise. Distinguish who pays the platform, who receives value, who fulfills, who controls quality and who bears exception costs.

An audience can be attractive only under a conditional service configuration. State the necessary location, time, partner readiness or fulfillment condition when supplied. Do not invent those boundaries or treat partner availability as permanent. Consider the customer's alternative when the matching or fulfillment condition fails, including doing nothing or going directly to another provider.

For a service-assisted offer, separate product contribution from assistance. A successful assisted case can support that bounded combined offer. It does not validate unaided adoption, broad capacity or repeatable economics without corresponding evidence.

## Distinguish rival mechanisms

Choose rivals that make different predictions, not a generic list of everything that might matter.

| Apparent signal | Plausible rival worth considering when supported | Discriminating episode or contrast |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Operators repeatedly request a feature. | The request is a proxy for a handoff that another actor controls. | Follow the last attempted handoff, including the receiving actor's required information and actual response. |
| The buyer says the offer costs too much. | Benefit is unproved, spend authority is absent, or implementation burden dominates the quoted price. | Reconstruct the last actual comparison, budget decision and implementation estimate; distinguish what was considered from what was purchased. |
| A trial is active. | Activity is evaluation work, exceptional assistance or curiosity rather than the intended recurring job. | Follow a normal job through its real outcome and identify who supplied the effort. |
| A customer renews. | Contracts, migration burden or a lack of alternatives explain retention. | Compare a recent renewal decision with achieved progress and the cost of switching; do not infer satisfaction from renewal alone. |
| No one uses an available feature. | They lack entitlement, exposure, readiness or a relevant job; deliberate non-use may be satisfactory. | Inspect supplied evidence of opportunity and the actual alternative before treating non-use as resistance. |

A rival is a hypothesis, not a replacement diagnosis. The useful result is a narrower claim and evidence capable of separating the explanations.

## Worked judgment example: field-service scheduling

This is a fictional teaching example, not customer evidence for a real assignment. The source labels below are illustrative supplied artifacts.

**Context.** A browser-based scheduler can consolidate work orders and let crews acknowledge changed assignments by text. The proposed value is fewer failed handoffs. The supplied packet contains:

- `N1, account A, coordinator note`: the coordinator reports re-entering changes into a shared calendar and calling crews. They want less re-entry. The note describes one recent late schedule change.
- `N2, account A, owner note`: the owner reports that revisits and missed customer appointments matter more than office typing time. The owner wants evidence that crews receive the correct change. No budget or price threshold is supplied.
- `L1, account A, pilot log`: fewer manual entries are recorded during an assisted pilot. Crew acknowledgment was not instrumented, and an employee helped maintain the schedule.
- `S1, summary`: says that buyers want automated scheduling, citing `N1`. It contains no additional interview.
- `N3, account B, owner note`: the existing shared-calendar process is reported to meet the current scheduling job. No recent missed visit is supplied.

**Tempting but unsupported conclusion.** “Owners are ready to buy because dispatchers save time; market to all field-service firms and emphasize automation.” This conflates roles, generalizes a selected case, counts a derivative summary as new demand and ignores the desired downstream outcome.

**Better explanation.** Account A may consider changing when schedule-change handoffs put appointments at risk. The coordinator receives a direct convenience benefit, but the owner's desired value depends on crew behavior and correct downstream service. The assisted pilot supports reduced recorded re-entry under its conditions; it does not yet establish fewer missed visits, unaided operation or payment intent. Account B is a meaningful no-change contrast, not a failed prospect.

**Actor map.** The coordinator reports the workflow; the owner states the outcome that matters to them. The crew controls a necessary adoption behavior. The evidence does not establish that the owner is the only payer or approver, so no extra authority is inferred. There is no reason to invent a procurement committee or a fear-of-change persona.

**Rivals.** One explanation is that failed communication is the binding problem, so acknowledgment quality would affect the decision. Another is that an adequate shared-calendar process already handles the job and the real constraint is staffing or exceptional rescheduling. A third, if the packet supports it, is that the pilot employee supplies the coordination benefit rather than the product itself. Do not add this third rival merely to meet a quota.

**Next proof.** Propose tracing the next comparable schedule-change episode from office change through crew acknowledgment to the customer appointment, including employee assistance and the existing alternative. A correct crew response with less total work would strengthen the combined offer's mechanism. Continued failure despite correct acknowledgment would narrow or challenge the mechanism. Success only with exceptional intervention would support, at most, an explicitly assisted offer. The proposal does not authorize access, employee work, contact or a live test.

**Correction handling.** If a dated release note later establishes that acknowledgments were available during a newer pilot, revise the current capability premise and identify which buying claims need rechecking. Do not erase the historical uninstrumented episode or claim its outcome was retrospectively proven. If a corrected account identifier shows `N1` and another note concern the same decision, correct the independent-decision count without discarding useful corroboration.

## Sparse context without a fictional committee

Suppose the user supplies only a product concept and an intended customer job. A useful provisional result can still say:

“The decision may become relevant when the supplied current process fails to deliver this progress. The person performing the job is a plausible chooser only if they can obtain and use the offer; payment or implementation may introduce another actor, but no such role is established yet. Keeping the current process is the leading known alternative. The most consequential unknown is whether the proposed capability changes the actual outcome enough to justify switching effort.”

Then specify a recent episode to reconstruct and the observable contrast that would reverse that explanation. Do not label every field unknown, invent a detailed persona, demand a completed ICP, or turn a conditional model into sales targeting.

## Using the result beyond this analysis

The explanation can inform a separately requested audience, message, offer or journey analysis. Carry the mechanism, source/version, counterevidence and unresolved dependency—not just a catchy customer label. A change in payment authority, current capability or partner cooperation may reopen several supplied conclusions at once. Flag them for revalidation; do not claim their owners or artifacts have been updated.
