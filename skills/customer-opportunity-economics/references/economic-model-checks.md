# Economic model checks

## Make the model inspectable

For each material result retain the source/field or supplied passage, input values, units, population/exposure, period, formula and executed output. State exclusions and missing-data treatment. A plausible formula or spreadsheet is not evidence that its inputs are true. If arithmetic cannot be executed, label the result unverified rather than presenting a claimed recomputation.

Use the minimum model that answers the question. A possible relationship is eligible exposed units × relevant events per unit × incremental value per event, but only when the factors describe compatible populations/windows and their conditioning is clear. Do not multiply independently reported averages that describe different segments or correlated quantities without justification. Prefer direct aggregate event data or a segmented model when available.

Keep stocks versus flows, people versus accounts, events versus customers, and annual versus monthly figures distinct. Mark currency and any conversion source/date. A percentage needs a numerator and denominator. An annualization must name its seasonality/stability assumption rather than quietly multiplying a short burst.

## Exposure is not addressable revenue

Document the steps actually supported between known population, eligible job exposure, problem incidence, accessible customers, adoption, sustained effective use and payment. They need not form independent multiplicative probabilities. If joins, overlapping cohorts or subaccounts create duplicate units, correct the unit before extrapolating. If no valid incidence evidence exists, keep the model conditional instead of borrowing an interview proportion.

Differentiate a reachable opportunity under present constraints from a hypothetical broader market. Do not invent adoption, capture or penetration percentages to complete a market-size template. A symbolic model and the one missing denominator can be more useful than an unsupported TAM/SAM/SOM diagram.

## Benefit channels and counterfactuals

For every benefit ask what would otherwise happen and who receives it:

- **Time:** usable capacity, overtime avoided or cash labor expense reduced are different outcomes. Reallocated time needs an attainable alternative use; a loaded wage rate does not make the entire time saving cash realizable.
- **Transactions/revenue:** incremental completed demand differs from transactions shifted between channels or periods. Revenue is not contribution margin. Do not add a retained sale and the same avoided loss twice.
- **Risk:** a supplied expected-loss calculation requires compatible probability, severity and exposure definitions. Rare-event anecdotes do not establish rates. Tail outcomes and downside can matter without a credible expected-dollar estimate.
- **Support/rework:** removing the same underlying error may produce several observed downstream savings; map dependencies before aggregation.
- **Quality/trust/control:** retain consequential nonmonetary evidence rather than inventing a dollar conversion.

Observed differences between users and non-users may reflect selection, exposure or timing. They do not establish causal incremental benefit. Label a scenario's assumed improvement separately from any observed level or experimentally supported effect.

Where overlap is unknown, show components as nonadditive and explain what event-level evidence would resolve it. Lower/upper bounds must follow justified constraints; an optimistic total is not a defensible upper bound merely because it is called one.

## Capture and cost

Distinguish customer savings, transferable existing spend, stated willingness, a specific offer response, actual payment and sustained net economic value. None automatically determines the next. Specify who can approve payment, budget cycle and constraints when evidence exists. Price/packaging options remain hypotheses for validation, not approved commercial policy.

Model costs at compatible units and periods. Include relevant ongoing human work, failed cases, integrations/partners, servicing variation, churn or nonpayment only with evidence or explicit scenario status. Do not use generic LTV:CAC, payback or margin thresholds as a pass rule. If a supplied business policy supplies a threshold, preserve its source and applicability.

## Sensitivity and 10x/100x

Anchor each scenario to a stated baseline and variable: customers, transactions per customer, complexity, geography or something else. Demand growth, market expansion and efficiency improvement are different scenarios. Show which relationships were held constant and why; ratios are not forecasts.

Vary inputs that matter to the conclusion using supported ranges or clearly labeled exploratory assumptions. Do not attach probabilities without a basis or claim arbitrary optimistic/base/pessimistic cases are statistical intervals. Explore correlated shifts and cost steps when material: a different customer mix, increased support burden, procurement constraints, partner dependence, increased failure exposure, or pricing pressure.

The useful output is a reversal condition: what would have to be true for the economics to become unattractive or compelling, and what evidence can test that condition. It is not an infrastructure design or authorization to scale.
