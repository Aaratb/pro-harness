# Claim Transfer and Correction Judgments

Use this reference when a promise depends on transferring proof across versions, audiences, offers or use conditions, or when a correction could invalidate an active claim. Apply the relevant judgments; do not require every task to produce a large evidence system.

## Follow the mechanism, not a blanket evidence grade

Evidence supports a particular proposition. Before transferring it, write down the source proposition, the proposed new proposition, and the difference that could break the connection.

| Inspected evidence | Strongest typical use | Missing bridge to a broader promise |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approved specification or roadmap excerpt | Described or planned capability and intended constraints | Delivery, release, customer eligibility and demonstrated behavior |
| Versioned authoritative release/availability record | Reported released state within the record's product, configuration and time bounds | Every customer's entitlement, actual exposure or successful use |
| Supplied technical test result | Behavior reported under the tested conditions | Production population coverage, customer usability and outcomes |
| Demonstration recording or transcript | Behavior shown or reported in that demonstration, preserving which medium was inspected | Independent self-serve use, repeatability outside that setup and sustained value |
| Customer use record | Specified behavior by eligible/exposed users in the recorded window | Broader adoption, causal outcome improvement or customer preference |
| Customer-reported benefit | A reported benefit for that source's situation | An independently measured effect, prevalence or causal attribution |
| Controlled or otherwise credible outcome evidence | The effect supported by its design, population and limitations | Transfer to materially different users, products, commercial conditions or periods |

These are not automatic promotions or a ranking of people. A release manager can authoritatively report a feature's release while knowing nothing about whether a particular buyer can use it. A customer can accurately report failed use despite the feature being deployed. A screenshot of a disabled button can support an access observation without proving the code does not exist.

Separate authoritativeness for **which question** from recency and source confidence. Where two permitted sources conflict, test whether they describe different versions, environments, entitlements, dates, definitions or actors. If the difference remains unresolved, preserve the conflict and draft a narrower claim; do not choose the convenient source or silently treat summaries as independent corroboration.

## Transfer boundaries that change commercial meaning

Evaluate the relevant differences and explain their consequence:

- **Product version or configuration.** A changed dependency can invalidate one demonstrated task while leaving other capabilities intact. An unchanged feature may retain its earlier scoped proof, but do not assume equivalence where the relevant behavior changed. A supplied rollback record can supersede current availability without rewriting earlier successful use.
- **Audience and use case.** Administrator success does not establish non-admin access; one region or plan does not cover all customers. A narrow counterexample can refute “everyone” while saying little about prevalence. Distinguish an excluded audience from an audience whose eligibility is simply unknown.
- **Offer, price and terms.** The same function may remain technically supported after pricing changes, while conversion or willingness-to-pay evidence no longer transfers. A free trial's completion does not establish acceptance of a paid annual commitment. Conversely, payment does not prove use or customer outcomes.
- **Message and exposure.** A response belongs to what the person actually saw, not later improved wording. A revised promise can change who enters the funnel and what they expect. Keep unexposed, exposed-but-ineligible, eligible-but-not-enabled, attempted use and successful use distinct where the evidence supports those distinctions.
- **Motion and assistance.** Concierge setup, training, partner configuration or sales involvement may be necessary complements. Preserve that support in the claim instead of relabeling the result self-serve. Evidence of a supported path is useful even when the unsupported path remains unproven.
- **Time and comparison.** Current capability does not erase earlier pain; historical pain does not prove present incapability. Compare equivalent periods and conditions for superiority claims. Unknown competitor behavior is not absence, and an omitted feature in an excerpt is not proof of uniqueness.

Do not reflexively invalidate all proof after any change. State which inference fails and why, what remains supported, and what minimal bridging evidence would permit the proposed transfer. An unchanged technical capability and an unproven new commercial offer can both be true.

## Numerical and outcome promises

Retain the metric definition, units, numerator/denominator, selection frame, exposure, period, exclusions and source locator necessary to interpret a number. Keep source reports distinct from recomputation. A supplied “50% faster” is not independently checked merely because it appears in a ledger.

When approved inputs and local calculation tools are available, run the arithmetic needed for the claim and report its inputs, method and result. Otherwise mark the check unverified. Do not substitute zero for missing counts, pool overlapping participants as independent evidence, or manufacture a denominator from the number of source documents.

Reduction in time, increase in throughput and percentage-point change are different claims. Even correct arithmetic does not establish that the product caused the change: sample selection, assistance, seasonality, workload and other interventions may explain it. A demonstration proving a task completed cannot substantiate a promised percentage improvement in customer outcomes. Preserve a useful scoped descriptive result where causality is not established.

An outcome claim also needs a mechanism: capability changes an activity or constraint, adoption occurs under specified conditions, and a consequential result follows. Missing adoption evidence should remain a missing bridge, not be filled with the intended value proposition. Detailed new collection, technical verification and outcome assessment require separately authorized work; identify the proof needed without dispatching it.

## Correct and propagate only within inspected scope

Use a compact correction trail when a current claim changes:

1. Preserve the original wording, source/version, time bounds and any supplied historical approval. Distinguish “incorrect when asserted” from “supported then, no longer current” and “never established.” Proposed corrections can identify a historical error, but must not silently rewrite its source record.
2. Locate the correcting evidence and its authority for the disputed proposition. State exactly which capability, audience, configuration or outcome inference changes, retaining material conflicts.
3. Replace or retract the unsupported current wording in the present deliverable. Give useful surviving wording where possible. A contradictory qualifier buried after an unchanged promise does not resolve the overclaim.
4. Trace each affected supplied dependent decision: eligibility, offer suitability, price/value assumption, message or copy version, motion, demonstration, enablement or readiness. Explain whether it needs narrowing, withdrawal or new proof. A dependent document not supplied is a revalidation destination, not an inspected artifact or an edit already made.
5. Preserve unaffected claims, historical experiences and current customer obligations. Name continuity or remediation decisions separately from restrictions on new market exposure. Do not treat a launch hold as technical rollback authority, permission to break a contract, or proof that all customers are affected.

An approval records who approved what, when and under which assumptions. It is not evidence that the assumptions remain true. Identify the present revalidation need without revoking or manufacturing historical approval records. Similarly, a recommended customer notice is not a notice sent, and revised draft copy is not a published correction.

## Worked example: a narrower present claim, intact historical evidence

The following is fictional teaching material, not evidence for a real product. Suppose a user supplies these labeled excerpts and asks to correct an upcoming Waypoint review-workflow announcement:

- **S1, release note v2.4, “Routing,” paragraph 2:** internal review routing is released for Team-plan workspaces when an administrator enables routing. Guest routing is described as a separate beta.
- **S2, demonstration note D7, “Setup” and “Observed result”:** two invited Team-plan teams completed internal-routing tasks after guided administrator setup. The inspected source is a facilitator's note, not the underlying recording or customer telemetry.
- **S3, interview I9, passage 12, before v2.4:** a Basic-plan user reported manually chasing reviewers and losing track of approvals. No outcome measurement is supplied.
- **S4, current availability bulletin v2.5, “Guest beta”:** guest routing is paused; internal routing remains available on eligible, enabled Team workspaces.
- **S5, supplied draft announcement M3, headline:** “Every team can automatically route internal and guest reviews on any plan and finish approvals twice as fast.” Its offer paragraph promotes a paid Team upgrade.
- **S6, supplied account note A4, “Commitment”:** an existing paid Team customer has an ongoing evaluation of internal routing. There is no supplied cancellation or entitlement-change authorization.

### Useful product-truth brief

“Internal review routing is reported available for eligible Team-plan workspaces with administrator enablement. Guided internal-routing use is reported in the supplied demonstration note; self-serve adoption and a quantified approval-speed benefit are not established. Guest routing is currently paused.”

This is useful current wording grounded in the inspected records, not certification of live availability. A suitable replacement promise in this draft is: “Route internal reviews in eligible Team workspaces after administrator setup.” Do not retain “any plan,” “guest reviews” or “twice as fast” as active promises. If wording suggests actual live state beyond the supplied bulletin, retain the bulletin's date/version qualification.

### Proportionate claim ledger

| Current claim | Judgment and useful replacement | Source and boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every team, any plan | Contradicted as written. Limit to eligible Team workspaces with administrator enablement. | S1, “Routing,” paragraph 2; S4, “Guest beta.” Basic-plan entitlement is not established by a Team upgrade offer. |
| Internal and guest routing now | Separate the claims. Retain scoped internal routing; retract current guest-routing availability. | S4, “Guest beta.” The paused guest beta does not negate internal routing. |
| Approvals twice as fast | Unsupported; remove the quantified promise. Describe the routing task without inventing an outcome improvement. | S2, “Observed result,” supplies a facilitated task report, not a duration comparison. S3, passage 12, reports earlier pain but no measured improvement. |
| Customers can succeed self-serve | Unresolved if proposed; do not infer it from guided setup. | S2, “Setup.” Request evidence of independent eligible-user setup/use only if needed for the intended promise. |
| Earlier manual-chasing pain was false | Unsupported inference; preserve the historical report. | S3, passage 12, predates v2.4 and concerns a Basic-plan user. Later Team functionality does not erase that episode. |

### Scoped downstream consequence

The supplied announcement M3 requires a current wording correction. Its paid-upgrade offer needs revalidation against the narrowed internal-routing promise and the setup conditions. The described “all teams” audience assumption is too broad; a narrower eligible audience may still be suitable. No supplied price evidence establishes whether the same price is commercially appropriate, so do not invent repricing or declare the offer invalid in every respect.

Readiness for a guest-routing promise is not established, but this does not automatically prohibit an internal-routing launch. Identify the owner decision and supporting availability evidence needed. If an enablement deck or landing page is mentioned but not supplied, flag it for claim revalidation; do not say it has been checked or edited.

The existing paid internal-routing evaluation in S6 remains an obligation to account for. A recommendation to hold guest-focused announcements does not cancel that work, revoke Team entitlements or authorize refunds. No outreach, publication, product check, entitlement change or workflow dispatch has occurred. The brief is a scoped correction proposal that the authorized owners can act on.
