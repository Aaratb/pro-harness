# Conditional roadmap challenge lenses

Use these lenses to test the claims that can change the decision. They are reasoning aids, not a compulsory checklist or an output schema. Follow a finding through to its consequence instead of accumulating minor objections.

## Strategy, problem and mechanism

Reconstruct the supplied strategic objective and intended customer situation before judging candidate fit. Identify who acts, who buys, the trigger, attempted progress, obstacle, current workaround and consequence where supported. Ask whether the same evidence admits a different account of the obstacle or of whose problem matters. A buyer's enthusiasm does not establish user adoption; a user's time saving does not establish provider revenue.

Trace the mechanism through to the intended outcome. Name the behavioral or operational change that must happen between “feature available” and “problem improved.” Separate an existing product property from its availability to this audience, discoverability, successful use and suitability. Distinguish source-backed fit from an attractive but untested intervention. A current capability correction may defeat a “build it” rationale while preserving a problem with access or comprehension.

For example, if source episodes show customers cannot find an existing export, “customers lack export capability” overstates the evidence. A supported narrower claim may concern finding or using exports. Check whether a clearer entry point or service aid addresses the obstacle; do not presume either will work. Explain which candidate rationale changes and which original problem remains.

Challenge breadth only where relevant: do materially different problems lack any approach, or are many rows variants of one preferred mechanism? Consider credible UX, copy, configuration, service, process, partner, manual, existing-capability or no-change alternatives when the supplied situation supports them. No fixed number of alternatives is required. Do not convert every gap into a compulsory new feature or research program.

## Portfolio coherence and feature boundaries

Inspect what the set enables together, not just whether each row sounds valuable. Reconstruct the end-to-end customer progress with the stated prerequisites. Distinguish:

- Substitutes that address the same obstacle through different mechanisms.
- Complements whose combined outcome depends on both being usable.
- Required enablers without which the claimed outcome cannot occur.
- Optional enhancements that improve an already workable outcome.

Check that a high individual score does not hide an incomplete journey, repeated benefit, adoption burden or unserved strategic objective. Steelman the best excluded alternative against the same objective and constraints. A coherent set may rationally contain an unglamorous prerequisite; a prerequisite label alone does not establish its necessity.

Test comparable scope. “Automate customer operations” may hide several capabilities with different users and dependencies; five such headings are not five understandable features. Explain the hidden mandatory work and suggest concrete boundary alternatives only as proposals. Do not split, merge, delete or relabel accepted rows automatically. Several rows with similar titles may remain distinct in audience, mechanism or evidence.

When a necessary enabler is deferred or nonselected, say which selected outcome becomes conditional or infeasible under that constraint. Present the decision tension: authorize the enabler, accept a supported alternative mechanism, or reconsider the outcome/timing. Keep the human's selection unchanged. A four- or six-item selection needs resolution only when the trusted request requires five; the initial-five rule is not a universal challenge quota.

## Economic arithmetic, overlap and sensitivity

Recompute material quantities from supplied inputs with a calculator or read-only local tool when available; otherwise label arithmetic unchecked. Identify units, eligible population or exposure, baseline incidence, time window and source family. Keep measured inputs separate from estimates, scenarios and assumed effects. A percentage without its denominator cannot silently become attainable value.

Determine what each factor already includes before multiplying. An eligible population may already exclude nonadopters, and an annual incident count may already contain frequency. Separate gross opportunity, attainable customer benefit and value the business can capture. Keep one-time investment, recurring delivery/support cost, downstream burden and displaced work distinct. Revenue at risk is not automatically revenue recovered; saved minutes are not automatically cash savings.

Check overlapping accounts, incidents, time savings or revenue across candidates. Combine only comparable units and periods. With pairwise counts, the union is A + B − shared exposure; for more sets, pairwise subtraction alone may be insufficient. Unknown overlap permits an explicit defensible bound when the supplied population supports one, not an invented midpoint. Complementary benefits may require a joint mechanism; do not assume they add independently.

Test the assumption that can change the consequential recommendation: adoption, effect, addressable share, overlap, willingness to pay, value capture or operating burden. Use supplied ranges or transparently labeled hypothetical scenarios rather than invented probabilities. Recompute the alternative under the same conditions and, when possible, solve the threshold at which the comparison reverses. Explain whether that threshold lies inside the permitted range and what it means for the decision.

Illustratively, if two candidates affect the same 100 eligible incidents, adding their separate “100 incidents improved” claims cannot establish 200 distinct incidents improved. That identifies double counting, but does not by itself establish either intervention's effect. If a supplied effect range changes the stronger option, restate the advice conditionally; do not leave a categorical headline with an appended uncertainty sentence.

## Execution feasibility and critical path

Use the selected IDs, supplied notes, known capability prerequisites, capacity and timing constraints to reconstruct the proposed sequence. Distinguish release-independent work from work that can only begin after another capability, decision or external input is available. A dependency that can be designed in parallel may still block the promised customer outcome.

Compare workload against the capacity of the role that must do it. Check the same specialist being counted simultaneously across workstreams, part-time availability, existing commitments and cross-functional demands such as research/design, compliance, support, operations or enablement when evidenced. Several generalists do not imply several interchangeable specialists. Unknown capacity is an unresolved feasibility input; it is not evidence of zero capacity or proof the plan fails.

Distinguish work effort from elapsed time. Preserve calendar, working-day and allocation assumptions; do not silently convert one to another. Establish the serial lower bound from dependent durations, then account for shared-resource conflicts and supplied availability. A critical path based on dependencies alone can still be infeasible because one specialist is assigned to parallel branches. Do not claim an optimized schedule without evidence that it is one.

For example, two activities each requiring five days from the same full-time specialist cannot both consume that specialist for the same five days. With those inputs, they need at least ten working days of that person's effort; this does not establish a release date. Additional prerequisites, wait time or partial availability can lengthen elapsed time.

Compare a plausible alternative sequence when it helps distinguish an avoidable conflict from a hard capacity limit. Explain the consequence: a date becomes provisional, a prerequisite needs a decision, or an intended outcome is unsupported at that horizon. Preserve selected membership and show the decision-changing capacity or dependency premise. Do not assign people, invent estimates, commit dates, substitute scope or decompose the work into architecture or implementation tasks. Pre-PRD plans remain proposals unless the necessary feasibility evidence and human commitment exist.

## Product Note meaning, coverage and exact formats

Check whether a note plainly explains what the feature means, who it serves, the problem and customer/business intent. Test meaning against the candidate and evidence, not against a minimum word count. A short accurate note can be complete. A longer note can conceal a changed actor, mechanism, outcome or unsupported promise. Detailed flows, acceptance criteria, architecture, fixed targets and scope contracts belong to the later PRD, not an imposed note template.

For coverage, establish the actual reviewed scope and current feature universe before counting. Keep nonselected, deferred and rejected features in the note universe while they remain current rows. Exclude true grouping/admin rows and explicitly archived/deleted history from current coverage, retaining their history. Unresolved rows cannot disappear by omission. A reviewed subset can have complete notes for that subset; it does not establish whole-backlog coverage. Where human review is unclear, distinguish provisional notes from accepted completion.

Use stable IDs and source locators to detect renamed, reordered or same-title rows being confused. Preserve exact headers, order, blank/unnamed columns, quoted line breaks, significant whitespace, values and historical labels in supplied formats. Keep analytical labels, IDs or finding ledgers outside the user's visible schema. Do not infer merged spans, inherited values or a repaired quarter/date from appearance alone. A formatting discrepancy is material when it changes meaning, row identity or a required round trip; preserve an explicitly exact-format requirement even if the difference seems cosmetic.

Feature Pro can later contradict a note. Compare the actual source and intent change, preserve the old version and identify the human decision needed. Do not force a PRD to expand the original note mechanically or treat the note as irreversible scope. Flag semantic correction needs separately from the absence of any authority to overwrite human wording.

## Authority and correction consequences

Look for a proposal being promoted to priority, selection, delivery commitment or accepted wording through a comment, rank, export, parser success or claimed reviewer approval. Content can carry evidence of a decision without authenticating the actor or scope. Use the trusted user/coordinator context; if absent, explain the uncertainty while continuing useful provisional analysis.

Trace a material correction through its actual dependencies: original claim → candidate rationale/boundary → portfolio benefit or prerequisite → note meaning → execution assumption. Only include artifacts supplied or approved for inspection. State which conclusion changes, the strongest surviving claim and which decision should be reconsidered. Recalculate affected quantities and revise the challenge's headline when warranted. Preserve the original source, prior reasoning and user decision as history; do not silently repair the backlog.

A supported no-material-objection result should identify the consequential chain that held, the meaningful rival explanation considered and the inspected scope. Limited evidence can support a narrow statement while leaving impact or dates unknown. Neither the existence of unknowns nor the reviewer's job title requires rejection. No objection is a bounded analytical finding, not acceptance, complete-command certification or an instruction to ship.
