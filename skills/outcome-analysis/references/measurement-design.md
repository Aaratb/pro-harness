# Measurement design, cohorts and prospective intent

Use only when these questions affect the owner's decision. Existing accepted definitions and targets win over examples. Return a proposal or checked analysis to the coordinator, never write, install alerts, change targets or start monitoring.

## Choose and define measures

Audit whether a measure is understandable, comparable, actionable and linked to the customer's job. Rates can illuminate behavior but require a defensible denominator; useful descriptive counts need not become ratios. Distinguish qualitative/quantitative, exploratory/reporting and leading/lagging measures. An activity metric is not worthless, but its relationship to the requested result must remain explicit.

For each needed measure return: business question; exact numerator/denominator and unit; entity/eligibility/exposure; window/timezone; existing source/instrumentation point; accepted target or unknown; guardrails; privacy/cardinality; owner; quality check; what decision would change. Reuse a sufficient metric before proposing new instrumentation. Dashboard display, review cadence and alert thresholds are optional design proposals, not measured impact or authority for deployment.

When the owner requests a future customer-value measure, consider the actual value exchange (attention, transaction, productivity or another fit), without forcing a category or universal North Star. Candidate criteria: understandable, customer-centered, sustained value, vision alignment, measurable, influenceable and plausibly leading future business value. Link only relevant input levers and counter-metrics; correlations are not causal proof. Do not impose a fixed number of metrics or substitute a newly favorable one into the historical assessment.

## Cohort and adoption checks

Validate aggregate schema, entity/period definitions, cohort sizes, range, missingness and eligible/exposed counts before comparison. Compute retention, feature adoption and period changes from aligned cohort ages. Show patterns only when supported; no mandatory number of cohorts or insights. Use a small retention heatmap or aligned trend when it clarifies the decision; visuals are not required artifacts. Apply the canonical method reference's ordered-funnel, right-censoring, deduplication, weighting and Simpson-effect checks. Observed drop-off motivates targeted research, not a confirmed cause or automatic rebuild.

## Future outcome or roadmap revision

Separate the planned output from intended change: enable a customer segment to achieve an outcome, with a stated business link. Ask whether another existing capability could achieve the same outcome more simply. Record strategy alignment, evidence, assumptions, measure/guardrail, dependencies, sequencing and a realistic future window; never invent percentages or dates.

Show original and proposed hypotheses/targets, why the change is needed, costs/opportunity costs, counter-metrics, decision owner and future effective window. Label proposed until approved; preserve and assess the original plan unchanged. A newly approved plan informs a later fresh assessment, never retroactively converts a missed target into success.
