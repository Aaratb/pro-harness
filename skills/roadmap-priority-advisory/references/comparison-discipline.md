# Comparing choices, not filling a scorecard

Use for multi-option, portfolio, quantitative or correction work. These checks deepen the entrypoint; they are not mandatory output sections or a new scoring framework.

## Compare like with like

Define the choice boundary before ranking it. An initiative with several customer capabilities, a small copy change and a confirmed defect can share a backlog but are not automatically comparable investment units. Preserve their identities and explain the difference. Offer a narrower capability boundary where useful without creating a hidden bundle of required work.

For each consequential option explain its current alternative, actor/job, mechanism, intended benefit, conditions, burden and evidence. Separate a capability the product demonstrably has from a candidate's proposed detail. A proposed timing, role, integration or automation mode cannot serve as proof that the current product already does it. Awareness, eligibility and successful use require their own support.

Distinguish constraints and obligations from preferences. If the user supplies a non-negotiable reliability, policy or contractual requirement, show how it restricts the feasible choices without inventing legal certification or silently launching remediation. If the fact is unknown, ask for the relevant owner/evidence instead of declaring an obligation. The ability to investigate a bug does not turn it into a new product capability.

## Compare coherent sets

Individual rank order does not establish a good portfolio. For a proposed first batch:

1. Trace the intended customer journey or outcome at capability level. Which proposed items make it possible, and where does progress stop?
2. Distinguish required enablers from useful complements and substitute mechanisms. State what supplied fact establishes a prerequisite; do not infer one merely from proximity in a sheet.
3. Identify overlap in beneficiaries, events and causal pathways. Two notification channels about one event do not provide two independent recovered outcomes. They may still offer reach, access or resilience benefits if those mechanisms are supported.
4. Expose capacity and sequencing tensions without inventing a schedule. Two individually affordable options may not fit together; shared resources may constrain the set even when total nominal effort looks small. Mark unknown cross-functional work as unknown rather than free.
5. Compare credible alternative sets when doing so changes the decision. Explain the strongest reason to retain an excluded feature and what evidence/constraint would make that alternative preferable. Do not enumerate every combination merely to appear exhaustive.

Name the resulting end-to-end benefit and remaining gap. A set is not coherent merely because all five items support the same slogan. Do not hide missing capabilities inside an oversized fifth feature to satisfy the count.

Human-selected IDs remain unchanged while this analysis happens. A missing deferred enabler warrants a proposal to reconsider scope, set, approach or timing—not auto-selection. If the requested set size is incompatible with the known outcome, show that tension explicitly. Selection, feasibility and readiness are separate judgments.

## Quantities that can support a recommendation

Keep a compact input ledger: source/field, quantity, unit, eligible population or episode, time window and observed versus estimated status. Choose an appropriate model for the actual decision, not a universal formula. For work-saving scenarios a useful structure is eligible episodes per period × adopted fraction × saving per effective use, minus offsetting work in the same unit/period. Do not reapply eligibility already present in the denominator or assume uptake is effective use without support.

Check conversions, signs and ranges with a local calculator/tool. Report negative net value if offsets exceed the estimated benefit; do not clamp it to zero. Use adverse/favorable endpoint combinations only when those joint scenarios are meaningful, and state dependence assumptions where they matter. A bound is not an expected value or confidence interval without appropriate evidence.

Do not subtract engineering days from recurring operator-hours or call released capacity payroll savings. Do not convert revenue to margin, opportunity to attainable sales, or customer value to willingness to pay without the required inputs. If a payback calculation is requested, match the relevant party, units, cost basis and horizon first. Preserve important nonmonetary downside rather than assigning a made-up price.

When combining candidates, identify overlapping exposure and outcomes before adding benefits. If overlap or joint effects are unknown, use separate nonadditive scenarios. Explicitly conditional calculations are useful; an unsupported point total is not.

Use a supplied scoring framework only when requested and the inputs mean comparable things. Show missing inputs and provenance; a missing score is not zero. Suggest possible methods or weights as options if asked, never silently choose weights for the user. Keep the framework's output advisory even when every cell is filled.

## What changes the advice?

Choose a load-bearing premise: eligible reach, uptake, effective benefit, support burden, a required capability, strategic audience, or a binding constraint. Solve a break-even comparison where possible, holding specified scenario anchors explicit. Check whether the threshold is feasible; an adoption fraction above one means that lever cannot reverse the choice under those assumptions, not that adoption will exceed 100%.

Compare sensitivity with the supplied plausible interval. A recommendation robust within that interval can still fail if the estimates, mechanism or objective are wrong. Do not make probability claims from scenario ranges. In a qualitative comparison, specify a discriminating observation rather than demanding a complete research program.

A changed material premise must propagate through the argument. Retain previous provenance, apply the corrected definition once, recompute dependent quantities and revise the recommendation if its rationale reverses. An existing capability may shift the opportunity toward access, discoverability, service or a narrower excluded segment; it does not prove the reported pain disappeared.

If the user still chooses the formerly weaker option, separate your advice from their decision and explain the reason they gave. Do not rewrite accepted priority or treat disagreement as invalid data. Keep any unresolved factual claim visible without substituting your own choice.

## Keep the output usable

Lead with the decision-relevant insight, not a long recital of frameworks. Explain comparisons in ordinary language and show enough arithmetic or source anchors to inspect the conclusion. Keep the original sheet intact; a side comparison can carry IDs, hypotheses and proposed sets. The final question should concern the unresolved human trade-off, not ask the user to reconfirm a choice already made explicitly.
