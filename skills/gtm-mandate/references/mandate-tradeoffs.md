# Mandate trade-offs and revisions

Use only the judgments relevant to the current brief. This is not a requirement to run a strategy workshop or complete every GTM phase.

## Conflicting objectives

First check whether apparent conflict concerns the same customer, product, decision, time window and unit. Acquisition and installed-base adoption may be separate initiatives rather than rival metrics for one. A short-term target may coexist with a longer-term ambition if the relationship and unmeasured interval are explicit.

For a genuine conflict, recover whose decision each objective represents and whether it was accepted, proposed or merely mentioned. Do not resolve ownership through document recency, seniority inferred from a title, majority vote or the model's preference. Compare the actual trade-off: scarce sales effort, support load, customer disruption, margin, evidence maturity or lost learning. Identify which lever would need to change and who must choose it; a scenario is not an adopted decision.

Avoid an attractive blended goal that hides incompatible work. "Grow revenue, engagement and trust" is not a resolution unless the brief explains what happens when one worsens to improve another. Preserve a supplied guardrail even if it makes the target harder. Unknown thresholds require calibration or a decision, not invented percentages.

## Thin evidence, useful direction

A concept can support a conditional customer-behavior hypothesis without proven demand. Name the situation, intended progress, plausible alternative, capability contribution and uncertainty. Explain what evidence would distinguish an adoption problem from a product-fit, buying, access or service problem. Do not use a message preference study to establish an independent need or treat a selected pilot as a market-rate estimate.

When no baseline exists, propose its unit, source and collection window plus a relevant interim learning question. A baseline request is not permission to query a database or install analytics. State what can proceed as analytical or draft work while the gap is resolved. Missing evidence need not stop useful framing, but it limits a forecast or outcome claim.

## Fixed dates and expanding promises

Establish what the date actually means: internal review, a small permitted pilot, availability to an eligible group, or a public announcement. Do not assume any can stand in for another. For a proposed pilot, identify product/manual delivery, assistance, service capacity, permissible promise, exposure cap and recovery needs before treating it as feasible. A small pilot may legitimately depend on help if that condition is disclosed and staffed; it does not validate unassisted use at scale.

Separate assumptions from commitments already made. A revised date or paused acquisition does not remove obligations to customers who paid, received entitlements, supplied data or have pending work. Name the affected obligation and the responsible-owner question; do not invent refund, deletion, contract or communication policy.

## Category education without invented category leadership

An owner may deliberately pursue market education or a new category thesis. Preserve that mandate as a strategic intent, not evidence that customers perceive the category, prefer its vocabulary, or view the company as its leader. Identify the learning mechanism: changed problem recognition, accurate comprehension, consideration or a meaningful buying/adoption action. Reach can measure distribution of a message but not acceptance of a category. Existing customer language and alternatives may challenge the thesis; carry that challenge upstream rather than rewriting strategy by fiat.

## Worked judgment: a proposed adoption milestone

Fictional supplied context: a team wants to announce an already-built scheduling feature at its conference. A product leader proposes doubling feature usage; support wants to reduce repeat setup calls. An admin export lists 90 enabled accounts but does not establish who attempted setup. Eight interviewees were selected from accounts that contacted support. No numerical target has been accepted.

A useful recommendation is a provisional adoption mandate: help eligible teams complete their first usable schedule without avoidable repeated setup assistance, while preserving service quality. This connects usage and support through a testable mechanism; it does not declare that setup difficulty causes all non-use or that the two proposed objectives are already aligned.

The next evidence should separate enabled, attempted and successfully scheduled accounts within a defined window, and inspect failed versus successful setup episodes. The eight interviews reveal support-seeking experiences, not a failure rate among 90 accounts. Conference announcement timing remains a separate decision requiring verified availability and truthful promises. If setup works well and users rationally do not need repeat scheduling, the mandate may need reconsideration rather than stronger promotion.

## Correct the current conclusion, retain history

Use a small dependency trail: source/definition -> mandate assumption -> current objective/success basis -> supplied dependent proposal. Record what remains supported and what must be reconsidered. Do not overwrite an accepted historical target to make observed results look successful. If the user prospectively changes the target, retain its effective date and separate earlier observations from the new evaluation basis. A revised brief is not proof that affected assets, approvals or source systems were changed.
