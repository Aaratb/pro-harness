# Study and evidence judgments

## Choose a method for the uncertainty

The method should distinguish an important explanation or assumption under realistic conditions. Examples are options, not a mandatory sequence:

- Unclear problem/behavior: neutral past-episode investigation, workflow observation or a scoped behavioral-data analysis.
- Unclear concept/message comprehension or task fit: unaided paraphrase, concept comparison or a realistic task walkthrough with observations of confusion, workarounds and failure.
- Unclear adoption/change burden: examine an actual decision or planned workflow transition, including approvers and displaced work.
- Unclear commitment/payment: inspect evidence of a specific offer and meaningful commitment under known terms, without assuming intent is payment.
- Unclear incremental effect: a suitably designed comparison or experiment, where warranted and separately authorized; observational work can identify hypotheses without establishing causal impact.

A planned prototype, concierge service, landing page, paid offer or experiment is not built/executed by this skill. Do not propose contacting, charging or deceiving people as if authorization and safeguards are already settled. Any design involving such operations must state what approval, disclosure/consent, burden and operational safeguards need separate resolution. Do not assert legal compliance.

## Define the study before seeing its desired result

Record the question, mechanism/rival, population and selection unit, exposure/comparator, instrument or concept version, outcome/guardrails, time window, analysis, missingness and interpretation rules. Match detail to stakes; a formative conversation does not need invented statistical machinery.

Criteria can be qualitative discriminators, measured changes or business constraints. Give the source and rationale for any numerical threshold, margin or sample-design choice. If an effect estimate or policy needed for calibration is absent, state the gap and propose pilot/calibration work. Do not quietly convert a proposal into an approved threshold. Avoid indefinite extension until a favorable result appears; proposed revisions should record the reason and what earlier evidence can still support.

When interpreting an experiment, inspect whether the supplied design and execution support comparability, exposure attribution, outcome measurement and the claimed uncertainty. Do not assume randomization, independence, adequate power or clean assignment because the document says "A/B." Account for repeated looks, multiple outcomes, contamination or clustered units when relevant; if suitable analysis is unavailable, limit the conclusion and request it rather than guessing a method or precision.

## Interpret evidence by the claim it supports

| Supplied signal | Potential use | What it does not establish by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Liking or positive reaction | Appeal under the presented framing | Correct comprehension, independent problem demand, adoption or payment |
| Accurate unaided paraphrase | Understanding of the presented promise by these respondents under this exposure | Relevance, belief in the promise, independent demand, adoption or payment |
| Stated intent | A reported intention under specific terms | Future completed behavior |
| Commitment | Willingness to bear the specified real cost/effort | Sustained use, payment or durable value outside those terms |
| Observed use/task completion | Behavior in the observed setting | Repeat adoption, comparative improvement or organizational acceptance |
| Payment | A transaction under known price, subsidy and conditions | Profitable delivery, renewal or realized customer benefit |
| Sustained economic/customer outcome | Value under the measured exposure and period | Causal attribution or transfer to other populations without suitable evidence |

This is not a universal ranking. A purchased pilot subsidized by a sponsor can be weaker evidence of organic demand than an unprompted recurring workaround. Establish who acted, whether they had authority, what terms/incentives applied, what alternatives existed and which claim is being assessed.

## Exposure and denominators

Keep unique participants/organizations, sessions, eligible/exposed users, questions asked, usable responses and outcomes distinct. Nonresponse, not asked, not understood and negative are different states. Preserve pre-concept versus post-concept material and prompted versus volunteered answers. Do not pool changed concepts/instruments silently or use post-pitch enthusiasm to confirm a preexisting problem.

Recruitment for contrast can reveal mechanisms but does not make observed proportions population estimates. A positive result from current champions, incentives or assisted completion may not transfer to new users or independent use. Name the transfer condition instead of dismissing all useful observations.

## A concrete message-comprehension study

First state the intended promise, audience/use context, actual delivery limits and the misunderstanding that would matter. Treat a draft's clarity problems as analyst hypotheses, not customer findings. A standalone study can begin without a finalized ICP or polished positioning artifact, but its audience assumptions remain explicit.

Design neutral past-episode discovery before showing a message when the same study also investigates the problem. Then record exactly what participants see or hear, message/concept version, other demo/coaching exposure, order and assigned role. Avoid explaining the promise until initial interpretation is captured. Prior exposure cannot be undone by labeling a later question "unaided."

Useful probes include: "What do you think this does?", "Who would use it, and when?", "What would you expect to happen next?", "What would you still have to do yourself?" and "What, if anything, is unclear?" Follow an actual paraphrase rather than offering the desired answer. Record spontaneous interpretation before asking about relevance, credibility, distinctiveness or liking. A respondent need not reproduce our wording; equivalent accurate understanding matters. Do not train participants until they can recite the intended message and call that first-exposure comprehension.

Compare observed interpretations with the intended and deliverable promise: correct meaning, partial meaning, material mistaken promise, unclear/not understood, or not asked. Preserve actual responses and locators, including favorable reactions paired with mistaken expectations. Diagnose whether a mismatch concerns audience, job/category, capability, outcome, timing, assistance or proof. Check whether this is a communication problem, an unsupported product promise, or both. Do not hide high-consequence false expectations in a blended clarity score.

In supplied results, report the actual denominator for each question and the unit (people/accounts/exposures). Separate selected champions from prospects and user responses from buyer authority. If interpreting comparison results, record order effects and prior concept exposure; do not assume a preference proves comparative effectiveness. Without a justified sampling/design basis, counts describe these responses, not a market percentage. Criteria need study-specific provenance; no universal readability target, pass percentage or participant count.

If revising a message, retain the tested version, source evidence, reason for revision and proposed new wording. New wording is an untested stimulus, not a corrected historical response. Do not pool different versions or claim improvement without a suitable comparison. A next study can ask whether the revised promise is accurately understood by the relevant roles without implying new capability. Plan separately authorized fieldwork, not campaign execution or publication.

## Corrections and inconclusive outcomes

A correction can affect the denominator, concept shown, observed behavior, respondent authority, price terms or premise itself. Reassess the corresponding inference; do not merely add a footnote to a still-active success claim. Preserve what earlier evidence legitimately supports, including a useful failure episode.

If several mechanisms remain consistent with the evidence, say so and identify the cheapest proportionate observation that could separate them without deciding a product roadmap. Inconclusive evidence is not a mandate to build, to continue forever, or to fabricate certainty. A no-new-product conclusion may follow when existing/service/process approaches sufficiently address the evidenced problem.
